Spatial devices

Fragment 04 · six probes
Dark shown on kraft, in context

Not colour studies. These test the things in the two plates that only exist in motion or in space — curvature, sourceless light, unresolvable depth, and one coil interrupting a lot of straight lines. Judge them as devices, not as layouts.

01

Fisheye

Move across it
The colour strip, bent. Space near the cursor dilates and space far from it compresses — the plate's convex ground applied to something the site already has. Nothing moves except the widths, so it stays a printed object rather than becoming a carousel.
02

The monument

Candidate for the intro slot
Hard wedges from one off-centre point, three punched ellipses, one coil. The coil is the only curve in a field of straight lines, which is the whole tension in the Fomenko. Deliberately underlit — detail arrives after a few seconds of looking rather than immediately. The small thing at the bottom is a three-joint arm, dwarfed: your work as the figure, so the scale relationship is made of your own subject and not borrowed.
03

Sourceless light

Static
Flat ellipses lying on the ground, no lamp anywhere. The figures cast shadows and the light still refuses to explain itself. This forces one amendment to the rules: pigment is flat, light is not pigment. A colour field never gets a gradient; a light pool has to have falloff or it stops being light.
04

The demo draws itself

Loops
LIVE — DRAG THE TARGET
One continuous stroke, then it hands over. This is where the hand earns its place: the loading state is the drawing, so the line is functional rather than decorative. Every demo on the site would open this way — and the same trick reads as a skeleton screen while the real thing boots.
05

Emphasis inversion

Static
the reason any of this is worth doing
The machine gun is drawn sharper than the man, and the cross is an aside. The tool gets clinical precision, dimensions, callouts. The thing that actually matters sits tiny in the corner at thirty percent opacity and is never explained. This is your rule about not stating your philosophy, turned into a composition rule instead of a prohibition.

Unresolvable ground

Four identical objects that disagree about where the light is. In the Fomenko you cannot find the ground plane, and that is most of why it is unsettling. On a page it is a real cost: shadows are how a reader knows what is clickable. My recommendation is to keep one consistent direction for anything interactive and break the rule only on decorative plates — but you should see the broken version before deciding.
